Settings decisions
Two groups, two label colours, never adjacent by accident. Evidence sources are sage-labelled and answer “what can REACH prove”; the analysis engine is violet-labelled and answers “who runs the reasoning”. The copy states the distinction in both places.
This is the only screen with provider logos , and they appear at 24–30px inside rows — never as promotional login cards, never on Home, never in a hero. Home now shows one engine status row that links here.
Engines are rows with radios, not cards. Selection, health, quota, and what each engine can and cannot see all live on the same row, so choosing is an informed act rather than a brand preference.
Every engine declares its data exposure — sees / never sees / retention — and the local option states its real cost (slower, weaker long-JD parsing) instead of being sold as strictly better.
Fallback is explicit and announced. One retry, then REACH-hosted, with the substitution named in the run summary. The product never silently swaps engines.
“What the engine cannot do” is a standing panel — no writes without accept / reject, no fabrication, no eligibility decisions, no influence on Opportunity Delta. It repeats the guarantees the rest of the product relies on.
Changing the engine warns about consequence, not about danger: the Delta may move a point or two, verdicts will not. Apricot, because it is attention rather than error.
Mobile is a grouped list at 48px rows with the active engine summarised in place, and the choice made in a sheet. Nothing is buried more than one tap from Profile.
Working Mode art density: a 38–52px engine object in the page head, 22–30px functional glyphs (engine, key, local, verified, citation, stale) and no scenery band. Provider logos are third-party marks, deliberately not pixel art, and are the one sanctioned exception to the pixel family — bounded to this screen.
