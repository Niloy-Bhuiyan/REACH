Roadmap decisions
A timeline, not a card grid. One rail with three node states — done (sage ✓), current (apricot ring, expanded), locked (grey, collapsed) — so progression is the layout rather than a decoration.
Only the current milestone is open. Completed and locked steps stay one row tall. That is what keeps a nine-step plan readable without pagination.
Every milestone names its artefact — 2 public repos, 1 portfolio entry, 2 CV lines — and the rail tracks 1 of 5 produced, so progress is evidence, not a percentage of activity.
Resources hang off the milestone that needs them. Free first at full weight in sage; the paid option is a dashed, quieter card headed “only if”, with the reason stated. There is no catalogue, no provider logos, no browsing.
The certificate verdict is a word plus a reason — Unnecessary here, because none of the 8 postings ask for one — with “how we decide” inspectable. Recommended / Optional / Low ROI use the same row.
The Delta stays auditable and re-counted per run, with the affected roles listed and their closing dates shown, so urgency comes from real deadlines rather than nudging.
The plan ends by applying. Step 3 is “update CV and profile, then apply”, and the queued Django roadmap is previewed but explicitly not started — one plan at a time.
Mobile recomposes around progression: header carries the blocker, gap type, progress bar and effort; steps are a collapsed list with the current one expanded; the sticky footer shows the delta and one action; full resources and the verdict live in a milestone sheet.
No XP, streaks, badges or levels — narrative density means one 92px map object, a 50%-opacity scenery bookend and 22–30px glyphs. Milestone node states use type and colour, not trophies. Still on the missing-asset list: the three-state milestone node, evidence-artefact glyphs, the certificate crest in four variants and a stepping-stone scenery band.
